The idea

Explore Your Block is one neighborhood game that turns into the right game for whoever picks it up: a sticker hunt for kids, a local history hunt for teens, an outing planner for adults.

Ollie the otter guides everyone along the same Duolingo-style path of places on your block. What changes is what each step holds, what is unlocked, and what is shared.

The country moment for the demo: the same 14-year-old gets different answers in two countries. In the US, a 14-year-old can turn on sharing. In Germany, the digital consent age is 16, so the same teen sees the feature locked with an "Ask a parent" button. k-ID supplies the real rule on the day.

Core loop

Walk to a place, find the thing, prove it, celebrate, unlock the next step.

  1. Pick the next step on the path. A card pops up with the task.
  2. Walk there. An invisible zone (geofence) around the spot knows when you arrive. For kids, the app only knows "inside the zone", never the exact location.
  3. Prove the find.
    • Kid: tap "I found it!" and answer one question from Ollie ("What color was it?").
    • Teen and adult: take a photo. The AI checks it matches ("Is this a mural?").
  4. Celebrate. Ollie bounces in, with confetti, a sound, and three score boxes (stickers, places, steps).
  5. Unlock. The step turns gold, the progress bar fills, the next step opens. Every 5 finds earns a badge.

Why no camera for kids: photos of kids, and of strangers on the street, raise privacy problems. Taking the camera away is itself a change the judges can see.

Demo script (2 minutes)

Open on the change, not the setup. Three phones (or browser tabs) logged in as three players, side by side.

  1. 0:00–0:15, the line. "Explore Your Block gets kids, teens and adults out exploring their own block. Same app, but it becomes the right game for whoever is holding it."
  2. 0:15–0:45, same screen, three players. The home path as an 8-year-old, a 14-year-old and an adult: drawn street vs map, stickers vs history vs cafés, camera off for the kid.
  3. 0:45–1:10, a kid find. Tap a step. Ollie says "Find something red!" out loud (Inworld). Tap "I found it!", answer Ollie's question, the celebration plays.
  4. 1:10–1:35, the country switch. Same 14-year-old, switch from the US to Germany. Sharing goes from open to locked with "Ask a parent" (k-ID). The parent taps yes; it unlocks.
  5. 1:35–2:00, an adult find + close. The adult snaps a photo of a café; AI confirms it; a local deal unlocks. Close: "One product. k-ID decides what is right for each player."

Backup: if live k-ID fails, a hidden switch sets age and country by hand so the demo still runs.
